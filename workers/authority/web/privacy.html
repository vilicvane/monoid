Monoid Authority

Privacy policy

Effective date: 28 October 2026

Monoid operates Monoid Authority, an optional bundle and time-proof service for Monoid cards. This policy explains the information this service handles. For privacy or deletion requests, email support@monoid.app.

Google sign-in

We request the openid email profile scopes. We verify Google's ID token and read your stable account identifier (sub), whether your email is verified, your email and your name. Your display name is your email only when Google marks it verified; otherwise it is your name, or “Google account”. We do not use your profile picture.

We use this information to identify the bundle owner, authorize access to that owner's bundles and show who is signed in. Your identifier and display name are held in the short-lived sign-in/session records described below. Google ID and access tokens are not retained after verification.

Signing in alone does not create a permanent profile. Your Google identifier is retained with any bundle records you create. A display label is kept until deletion only if you explicitly set one; the default Google email or name is not automatically saved as a permanent label.

We do not sell Google user data, use it for advertising or share it for unrelated purposes. It is used in your Monoid app and handled by the service providers described below as needed to operate the service. Monoid Authority's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

GitHub sign-in

We read your numeric GitHub user ID and login. With the read:org scope, we read organization memberships to find organizations where you are an active Owner. We use the organization's numeric ID and login, and recheck your identity and membership when you access organization bundles or labels.

Your GitHub OAuth token is encrypted in the sign-in flow, handoff code and session records. It is used only for this sign-in and its organization checks, never sent to the Monoid app, and is not kept as a long-term credential. Expired copies are removed through the cleanup process below. Organization memberships are not saved as a permanent list.

Wallet sign-in

We receive your Ethereum address, a Sign-In with Ethereum (SIWE) message and its signature to verify that you control the wallet. The issued challenge, including the message and address when already known, is stored temporarily. The submitted signature is processed but not saved by the service.

Ordinary wallet signatures are checked by the service itself. When a signature needs contract-wallet verification, the service queries the configured Ethereum RPC provider, currently PublicNode. That request can include the address, message hash, signature and contract-verification data. No wallet private key is requested.

The wallet chooser loads Reown AppKit in your browser and can use WalletConnect's relay to connect to your wallet. Reown is a replaceable connection provider; it does not determine your owner ID. Wallet software and connection providers may keep their own connection information.

Bundle records and display labels

We keep each bundle's owner source and ID, chosen name (or an empty name), fixed peer-extension policy and creation time. We also keep any display label you explicitly set, keyed by its owner source and ID. These records have no automatic expiry and remain until deletion.

Records are returned to your Monoid app when you are authorized to access them. Organization labels and bundles are shared with its other authorized Owners; wallet labels and bundles are available to anyone who can prove control of that wallet.

Card public keys used to encrypt issued bundles and challenges used to sign time proofs are processed for the response, but are not stored in the service's database. The service does not store card private keys, OTP secrets or funds. Individual bundle keys are derived when needed from a service master secret, the owner, the name and the peer-extension policy; they are not stored as individual keys.

Short-lived sign-in data

  • Sign-in requests and browser flows expire ten minutes after the request starts. OAuth state, nonce and PKCE records expire with that flow.
  • A wallet challenge can be used for up to five minutes, or until the flow expires, whichever comes first. The SIWE proof itself carries a fifteen-minute expiry.
  • The one-use code returned to the app expires after sixty seconds, or at the verified identity's expiry if earlier.
  • A session expires after five minutes without successful authentication. Each authenticated request renews that window, but never beyond fifteen minutes from identity verification or the earlier provider/proof expiry. Signing out removes that session record.

Consumed requests, codes and challenges are removed when used; Continue or Cancel removes the browser flow. Expired records cannot be used, but may remain in storage until an hourly cleanup alarm runs. Other outstanding attempt records can remain until their own expiry and cleanup. Cleanup timing depends on the hosting platform; expiry is enforced even if cleanup is delayed.

The browser uses a flow-specific, HttpOnly sign-in cookie with at most the remaining ten-minute flow lifetime, and tab-scoped session storage to resume the flow. Completion or cancellation clears the cookie and that tab's flow reference. Wallet proofs are held in page memory and discarded on return or cancellation, when wallet disconnect also starts. This does not sign you out of Google, GitHub or your wallet software; those browser sessions may persist.

Security, rate limits and diagnostics

The public service uses HTTPS. Verified identities, GitHub tokens and OAuth attempt secrets are encrypted by the application before storage. Random bearer tokens, handoff codes, flow lookups and browser-cookie proofs use hashed lookup keys or proofs. Not every field is hashed or separately encrypted: routing metadata, wallet challenges, owner IDs, names and bundle policies are database fields. Cloudflare encrypts the service's stored data at rest.

API requests use your IP address as a transient key in Cloudflare's rate limiter. Bundle issuance also uses an account-based rate-limit key. The service does not save IP addresses or those counters in its database.

Stored request logging is disabled. The service emits limited provider-error diagnostics for live troubleshooting: a provider, status and bounded error fields with known credentials redacted, or the category of a failed Google token check. It does not write these diagnostics to its database. Operators can view live logs; the hosting platform's request metadata may include request URLs. Disabling stored request logs does not mean that Cloudflare or sign-in/connection providers collect no operational data.

Service providers

  • Cloudflare hosts the service, serves these pages, stores service data and applies rate limits. It processes network information such as IP addresses to deliver and protect the service.
  • Google or GitHub handles the sign-in you choose and receives the authorization and token-exchange requests. GitHub also handles organization-membership checks.
  • Reown/WalletConnect provides the browser wallet-connection UI, wallet metadata and relay services when you choose Connect wallet.
  • The Ethereum RPC provider, currently PublicNode, processes contract-wallet verification queries.

These providers process the information needed for their part of the service under their own policies. We do not send your Google or GitHub tokens to Reown/WalletConnect or the RPC provider. Public home and policy pages load no sign-in or wallet SDK and no third-party resources.

Deletion requests

Email support@monoid.app to request deletion of stored display labels and bundle records. We may ask you to prove control of the account, organization or wallet so that we do not delete another owner's records. Do not email passwords, PINs, private keys or sign-in tokens.

Deletion removes the live records we hold for that owner. It does not remove bundles or data already on cards. It also does not revoke those bundles: the same owner, name and policy can produce the same bundle key again while the service master secret is unchanged. Email correspondence about your request is separate from the service's sign-in records and is used to handle your request.

Children and policy changes

This service is not directed to children under 13.

We announce policy changes on this page by updating the effective date. If we intend to use Google user data for a new purpose, we will provide notice and obtain any required consent before doing so.
